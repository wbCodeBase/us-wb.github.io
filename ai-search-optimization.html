01 The basics

What is AI search and how does it work?

AI search combines natural language processing (NLP), machine learning (ML) and large language models to return faster, more personalized and more relevant answers. Rather than matching exact keywords, it reads the context, intent and meaning behind a query.

Take the query "best barbecue restaurants in Texas." A traditional search engine behaves like a librarian, sorting pages by keyword matches and link popularity:

But AI search can go a step further.

AI search behaves more like a well-connected local. It works out what you actually mean, pulls from many sources at once and gives you a single, synthesized answer, often with follow-up suggestions and citations.

How AI search works, step by step

AI search runs on several layers at once. Every answer passes through the same seven-stage pipeline:

1Data collection 2NLP 3Machine learning 4LLMs 5Predictive analytics 6RAG 7Feedback loops

  1. Data collection

    AI models pull information from a wide range of sources: web crawling, structured databases, documents, user interactions, real-time news feeds and multimedia. That breadth lets AI search maintain an up-to-date knowledge base. Here's Gemini drawing on several sources for one answer:

  2. Natural language processing (NLP)

    NLP turns unstructured text into structured data the AI can analyze, so models grasp context, intent and meaning and handle conversational, multi-part questions. It involves:

    • Tokenization: splitting text into words or phrases
    • Lemmatization: reducing words to their base form
    • Part-of-speech tagging: labeling nouns, verbs and adjectives
    • Entity recognition: spotting people, places and organizations

    Ask Google AI Mode, "Will I need an umbrella in Seattle this weekend?"

    You never typed "weather" or "rain," yet it knows this is a weather question. It reads intent, not exact words.

  3. Machine learning (ML)

    Machine learning lets AI search sharpen relevance and make decisions without human intervention, using three approaches:

    • Supervised learning: trained on labeled examples.
    • Unsupervised learning: finds patterns and clusters in unlabeled data.
    • Reinforcement learning: improves through feedback on what users click and ask next.

  4. Large language models (LLMs)

    LLMs such as GPT and Gemini bring contextual understanding and semantic analysis. They interpret the deeper meaning of a query, handle nuance and follow-up context, and respond in human-like conversation. Ask Gemini, "What's the safest car for a family of five?"

  5. Predictive analytics

    Predictive analytics studies patterns in past searches and behavior to power autocomplete, predictive query expansion and content that matches historical intent. Someone who often searches vegan recipes will see suggestions like "quick vegan snacks."

  6. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

    RAG pairs the model with real-time retrieval. It runs vector searches across indexed sources, then writes an answer grounded in what it found. That reduces hallucinations, and it's why ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity show citations. This is where your brand earns visibility, even without a #1 ranking:

  7. Continuous learning & feedback loops

    Every query and interaction feeds back into the system: ranking logic, training data, autocomplete and source weighting are constantly re-tuned. What gets cited today can change next month.

03 Zero click

Are zero click searches killing SEO?

Short answer: no. AI answers are not the end of search engine optimization. They're creating new openings for brands that adapt. But nobody is immune:

Case study

HubSpot's traffic collapse

In 2024, HubSpot reported losing roughly a third of its organic traffic after Google rolled out AI Overviews. For years it was the benchmark for content-led SEO. If it can happen to HubSpot, it can happen to anyone.

Source: Search Engine Land
~33%organic traffic lost
Millionsof monthly visits affected

Over the next few years, LLMs are expected to deliver as much business value as traditional search, and then overtake it.

So the goal of SEO is shifting. Instead of chasing rankings and traffic alone, brands now need AI trust and visibility. Here's why that's an opportunity:

Think of it as a digital credit score. AI systems don't surface content just because of keywords or backlinks. They weigh the overall trustworthiness of your presence: brand authority, topical expertise, technical health and consistency across the web.

Zero click answers still depend on third-party sources. If your site publishes high-quality, relevant information, it shapes the AI's answer even when nobody clicks.

Showing up in AI citations again and again builds familiarity and trust, which pays off in branded search and direct traffic over time.

High-intent users still click, especially on comparisons, product reviews and long-tail queries. Well-structured content still earns that traffic.

05 GEO

What is generative engine optimization?

Generative engine optimization (GEO), also called AI SEO, is optimizing your digital presence so AI platforms see, trust and cite you. Traditional SEO ranks pages in SERPs; GEO helps LLMs understand and use your content when they answer questions.

What's changed

  • Keywords became prompts. People now write the way they talk. ChatGPT prompts average around 23 words.
  • Intent beats keywords. Winning content covers whole topic clusters and anticipates follow-up questions.
  • Entities over pages. AI thinks in people, places, things and concepts, not URLs.
  • Citations over rankings. The goal is to be the source AI references.

What hasn't changed

  • Human nature. Curiosity, problem-solving and decision-making still drive every search.
  • Expertise and authority. E-E-A-T matters even more; AI leans on credibility signals.
  • User experience. Speed, mobile-friendliness and clear navigation still shape success.

06 The playbook

How to optimize for AI search

Rather than fighting AI answers, use them to grow your brand's presence. We've been testing AI search strategies since answer engines first appeared. Here's what that work has delivered:

The 5-step playbook

01Build deep topical authority 02Cultivate brand reputation 03Make your site technically flawless 04Build authoritative backlinks 05Maximize multimedia presence

  1. Build deep topical authority

    AI models favor sources that show consistent, in-depth knowledge over surface-level coverage.

    • Share analysis, case studies and first-hand experience.
    • Publish long-form content that answers follow-up questions.
    • Add FAQs, definitions and summaries AI can easily extract.
    • Refresh content regularly with new data.

  2. Cultivate a strong brand reputation

    AI weighs brand signals from across the web, including reviews and third-party mentions.

    • Manage your Google Business Profile and directory listings.
    • Keep name, address and phone (NAP) consistent everywhere.
    • Ask for, and respond to, reviews on Google and industry sites.

  3. Ensure your website is technically flawless

    AI crawlers must be able to access, parse and cite your pages. Technical SEO is still a core AI strategy.

    • Implement schema markup and structured data.
    • Fix crawl errors; improve speed and Core Web Vitals.
    • Make every page mobile-responsive.

  4. Build authoritative backlinks

    Links and mentions from reputable sites tell AI your content is trustworthy. Digital PR earns them where AI models learn.

    • Publish original research others will cite.
    • Contribute to high-authority publications in your niche.
    • Use Ahrefs or Semrush to close backlink gaps with competitors.

  5. Maximize your multimedia presence

    AI search is multimodal: it reads video, images and audio as well as text.

    • Publish YouTube tutorials, demos and interviews.
    • Embed video summaries in blog posts.
    • Keep branding consistent across every platform.
